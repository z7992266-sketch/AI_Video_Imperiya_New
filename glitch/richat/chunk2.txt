Here's what geologists think happened. About a hundred million years ago, in the age of dinosaurs, molten rock rose from deep below. It never broke through like a volcano. It stalled underground, and lifted the flat layers of rock above it into a dome, like a blister rising under paint. Hot fluids then flooded the cracks. They dissolved rock at the center and left a jumbled breccia up to forty meters thick. Minerals from that hot-water event have been dated to about ninety-eight million years ago. Carbonatite dikes and a kimberlite plug, rocks that come from deep magma, date to around the same time. Then came the patient part: erosion. Over tens of millions of years, wind, sand and ancient rivers sliced the top off the dome. Hard quartzite layers resisted and stood up as circular ridges. Softer layers wore away into valleys. Slice the top off a layered dome, and you get rings, with the oldest rocks in the middle and younger ones toward the edge. That's the eye. So, not a crater. NASA's Earth Observatory calls it a deeply eroded geologic dome. And in twenty twenty-two, the International Union of Geological Sciences put it on its first list of one hundred geological heritage sites on Earth. Mystery solved? Not quite. Because the strangest thing about this eye isn't the rock. It's who was standing inside it, long before anyone looked down from space. But first, the theory that made this place go viral. Atlantis. In two dialogues written around three hundred sixty BC, Plato described a city built in rings, rings of land and rings of sea, that sank in a single day and night. Some online videos claim the Richat Structure is that city. Concentric rings. Case closed? Here's the problem. Plato put Atlantis on an island in the sea, beyond the Pillars of Hercules. Richat sits hundreds of kilometers inland, on a desert plateau. Its rings are natural layers of rock, not walls. No city ruins, no canals and no harbor have ever been found there. And most classical scholars think Plato invented Atlantis to make a point. So, for now, Atlantis stays where it belongs: in the legend file.
